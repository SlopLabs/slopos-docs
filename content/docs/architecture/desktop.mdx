The SlopOS desktop is meant to read as one product: the window frames, the
dock, the lock screen, the installer and every app use the same colours, the
same type, the same spacing and the same words. You get that without trying
when you build an app from **appkit**, SlopOS's toolkit, because the toolkit's
components carry the look and the behaviour, and an app has no way to name a
colour or a font size of its own. What it doesn't promise: there is one dark
theme, one screen scale and no way for a person to choose another yet.

This page describes the theme, the components, the checks that hold apps to
them, the parts of the desktop the compositor draws, and the apps that ship.
How pixels and input travel between apps and the compositor is on
[Windowing](/docs/architecture/userland-windowing); writing your own app is
[Write an app](/docs/development/writing-an-app).

## Where it comes from

None of the ideas here are new. Colours are named by the surface they sit on
and by what they mean, as libadwaita, GNOME's toolkit, names its own (window,
view and card colours; accent, destructive, success, warning and error). The
contrast rules are WCAG 2.2's: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for the edges of controls
and focus indicators. Icons are glyphs of Lucide, an open icon set shipped as
a font. App icons follow GNOME's and Apple's guidance: a few simple filled
shapes, no shadows of their own. An app is a function from its state to a
view plus a function that applies messages, which is the Elm architecture.
And failing a raw colour where a named one belongs is what lints such as the
`RawColor` check for Android layouts do, except that here it fails the build.

## Why the look is enforced rather than suggested

Before the theme existed, each app picked its own colours and sizes. Page
titles came out the same size as the paragraph under them, a disabled option
dimmed its title but not its reason, and one app had three names: *Editor* in
the dock, *Sloped* in its title bar and "SlopOS editor" in its About box.
Nothing about this is hard to fix once found, but finding it means looking at
every screen at every width after every change, which nobody does. So SlopOS
moves the decisions into code (a theme of named roles and components that
apply them), makes the shortcuts fail the build, and checks the result by
laying every screen out off screen and measuring it.

## The theme

`theme-core` holds every design value the desktop uses, and both appkit and
the compositor read them through it. Nothing else defines a colour.

- **Colours are roles.** A surface (`Base` for a window's content, `Raised`
  for cards, popovers and dialogs, `Sunken` for fields and lists, `Chrome`
  for title and menu bars), a text *tone* (`Normal`, `Secondary`,
  `Disabled`, `Accent`, `Danger`, `Warning`, `Success`, `Info`), and the
  edges, focus ring and selection. Every status hue exists twice: a light
  *ink* for text on a surface, and a deep *fill* for white text on it, which
  is how a filled button or a danger button is drawn. Hover and pressed are
  derived from the role, never listed per control: a filled control lightens
  under the pointer and darkens while pressed.
- **Text has roles too.** Each role is a size, a weight and a line height:

  | Role | Size and weight | Used for |
  |---|---|---|
  | `Hero` | 64 px semibold | the lock screen's clock, nothing else |
  | `Display` | 24 px semibold | a page that is one number |
  | `Title` | 18 px semibold | a page's title |
  | `Heading` | 15 px semibold | a section, the menu bar's app name |
  | `Body` | 14 px | text |
  | `Label` | 13 px | controls, the dock's labels |
  | `Caption` | 12 px | small print under something |
  | `Mono` | 13 px | code and command lines |

- **Space comes in steps** of 4 px (`space::XS` 4, `SM` 8, `MD` 12, `LG` 16,
  `XL` 24), with named insets for a page, a section, a card and a control.
  Controls have 6 px corners, cards and windows 12 px. A focused control's
  edge is 2 px rather than 1 px, so focus shows by more than a change of
  colour.
- **The chrome's own values** live there as well: the wallpaper's gradient,
  the glass of the dock and the menu bar, the rims and shadows of windows,
  eight app tile hues and seven distinct series colours for charts.

There is one table, a dark graphite one. A light theme would be a second
table, with no change to any app.

Interface text is set in Inter, code and the terminal in JetBrains Mono, and
icons in Lucide, all loaded at run time from `/usr/share/fonts`. They are
never compiled into a program, because the fonts' licences and SlopOS's own
could not both govern one binary. Every glyph in a line sits on one baseline,
pairs are kerned from the font's own table, and measuring, fitting and
drawing share one layout, so text that measured as fitting draws as fitting.

## Components

An app describes its window as a tree of *components*, each of which says
what a thing is rather than how to draw it: a `Page` (a title, a sentence about
it, content that scrolls and a row of buttons under a separator), a
`Section`, a `PropertyList` of names and values, a `Notice`, a `ButtonRow`, a
`List` of `ListRow`s, a `Sidebar`, a `Toolbar` of `IconButton`s, a
`SearchField`, a `StatusBar`, a `Tag`, an `EmptyState`, a `Disclosure`, a
`Dialog`, a `Graph`, and `Text` in a role and a tone. For lists of many
things and documents there are tables, trees, a code view, tabs, menus and a
zoomable picture. The appkit guide in the source tree, `appkit/GUIDE.md`,
says which to use when.

What makes them worth using is what comes with every one of them:

- **Text that doesn't fit is cut with `…`**, at its end or, for a path or a
  file name, at its start, and the whole text shows in a tooltip. Wrapped
  text never loses a word.
- **Every control has its states.** It lights under the pointer, shows when
  pressed, can be disabled and shows that too. Focus is one ring around the
  control, or around the row the keyboard is on in a list, table, tree or
  menu.
- **The keyboard works the same everywhere.** Tab and Shift+Tab move focus.
  Enter and Escape that no control took press the confirming and the back or
  cancel button of the page or dialog in front, and Enter never reaches a
  destructive button that way: only one that has focus takes it. A dialog
  keeps the keyboard and the pointer until it is answered.
- **Every control and every row is at least 28 px across**, so a pointer can
  find it.
- **Windows describe themselves.** An app states the size its window opens
  at, the least it can be laid out at, and whether it resizes, and the
  compositor holds it to that. A window showing a document is titled
  `<document> — <App>`, any other by the app's name alone, and that name is
  the one its dock entry uses.
- **Refusals say why.** When the computer refuses to open or change a file,
  appkit works out the reason from what the kernel says about the file and
  the filesystem it is on (`statfs` and the file's flags, never its path):
  the system's own file, an
  extension's, one locked against changes, a disk that can't be written, or
  permissions. The app says which.

The widget gallery (`widget_gallery`, or `widget_gallery --page <name>` for
one of its pages) shows every component in each of its states.

## How the look is checked

Three kinds of check hold apps to all this, none of which needs anyone to look
at a screen.

**The theme's own tests.** `theme-core`'s host tests compute WCAG 2.2's
contrast ratio for every pair the desktop can put together: body and
secondary text against every surface, hovered and selected rows included, at
4.5:1; status inks on plain surfaces at 4.5:1; white text on every fill in
every state at 4.5:1; and edges, the focus ring, disabled text and an app
icon's glyph against its tile at 3:1. Disabled text is held to 3:1 although
WCAG asks nothing of inactive controls. Another test checks that every icon
the code names is a glyph of the shipped font.

**The layout audit.** `appkit::audit` lays a tree out at a given size, paints
it off screen and measures what came out. It reports text cut by an edge
instead of an ellipsis, siblings that overlap, a child outside its parent,
text over text, a row whose texts don't share a baseline, a control whose
focus doesn't show all the way round, a control under 28 px, and text under
its contrast floor against the colour actually behind it. `appkit_test` holds
every component and every gallery page to it at 360, 640 and 960 px wide,
after first planting each kind of mistake to see that the audit finds it. The
consent dialog, the installer's pages, the System Monitor and the compositor's
own screens are audited the same way.

**Gates on the source.** `scripts/check_ui_literals.sh` fails an app that
constructs a colour or gives a spacing or an inset a number. A file whose
colours are its content, such as the editor's syntax palette, is exempt by a
line in `scripts/gates/ui-literals.txt` that says why.
`scripts/check_app_deps.sh` fails an app under `apps/` that depends on
anything but appkit, the editor's core and the image decoder, so no app
reaches past the toolkit into the kernel's interface or the toolkit's
internals.

## Words

Where words are data, they are checked too. The glossary in
`theme_core::words` lists what a screen a person acts on never says, and what
it says instead:

| Never | Instead |
|---|---|
| machine | computer |
| drive | disk |
| medium | installer |
| volume, region | partition |
| reboot | restart |
| halt, shut down | power off |
| admin | administrator |
| end session | log out |
| switch person | switch user |
| program, client | app |
| boot order, firmware entry | startup order |

A *label* (a button, a menu entry, a heading) starts with a capital and has no
full stop; a *sentence* starts with a capital and ends with a full stop or a
question mark; neither names a command-line flag or writes an ellipsis as
three dots. Buttons are verbs, and an ellipsis marks one that asks something
further (*Restart…*). Everything uses sentence case, unlike GNOME's
guidelines, which capitalise headings and buttons. Memory and file sizes are
binary (`512 bytes`, `1.5 KiB`, `8 MiB`), and disk sizes decimal, as a
drive's label gives them. Host tests hold privd's policy, every page of the
installer and the compositor's menus and screens to these rules.

## What the compositor draws

The compositor draws everything on screen that isn't an app's content.

<DesktopAnatomy />

- **The desktop** is the theme's wallpaper, a dark gradient lit softly from
  above, drawn at the screen's size and dithered so it shows no bands. A
  picture named by `SLOPOS_WALLPAPER` covers it instead.
- **Window frames** are rounded at all four corners (square once
  maximised), with a dark hairline just outside and a light one along the
  top, and a shadow that is half as dark on a window without focus. The
  close, minimise and zoom buttons are coloured discs that show their glyphs
  while the pointer is over them and turn grey on a window without focus. A
  resize takes a press up to 8 px outside the frame. Zoom fills the space
  between the menu bar and the dock, or gives the window the nearest size its
  limits allow; pressing it again puts the window back.
- **The menu bar** is a strip of glass across the top. On the left,
  SlopOS's mark opens the system menu and the focused window's app is named;
  on the right, the network item opens the network panel and the clock shows
  the time as `HH:MM`, in UTC until SlopOS has time zones, with the date a
  click away.
- **The system menu** names the person and offers *Lock screen*, *Switch
  user…*, *Log out…*, *Restart…* and *Power off…*, and *Restart desktop…*
  under a notice once a new base has changed the desktop's own programs.
  Each entry that ends something asks first, with *Cancel* focused.
- **The dock** floats as a pane of glass above the bottom of the screen. It
  holds the pinned apps, then the running apps that no entry names, a dot
  under each app that runs, and each icon grows a little as the pointer
  nears and is named in a label above it.
- **The lock and login screens** dim the wallpaper and show the time and
  date, the person's initial in a circle and their name (or, on the login
  screen, the people as rows, five at a time), a password field and *Unlock*
  or *Log in*.
- **Questions**, the consent dialog among them, are drawn over everything:
  a card centred over the window of the app that asks when it has one, its
  headline
  `<App> wants to <do something>.`, the app's icon, what follows from
  allowing it, *Cancel* and a button naming what it allows, filled with the
  danger tone when the action destroys something.
  [Privileged actions](/docs/architecture/privd) explains how it guards what
  you type and click.

The frames and the dock are painted directly every frame, but the
compositor's other surfaces (the lock and login screens, the system menu, the
panels and the questions) are appkit trees like any app's, laid out and
painted into the compositor's own buffer (`appkit::embed`), so they are built
from the same components and audited the same way.

[Sessions and login](/docs/architecture/sessions) covers what locking,
switching user and logging out do.

## App icons

Every app icon is one white glyph on a rounded tile in one of the theme's
tile hues, lit slightly from its top-left corner and nothing more: no sheen,
no rim, no shadow, since the dock casts the shadow. The shipped icons are
drawn by code (`just icons`, `tools/icons/src/art.rs`) from a few filled
shapes on a 64-unit grid, each inside a keyline of the same area, with depth
only from a shape behind at 60% white or detail cut out of a white shape.
They ship at 64 px and as `<id>@2x.png` at 128 px, and a host test fails when
a shipped PNG is not what the code draws. An app that ships no icon is shown
as a glyph of the icon font on a tile whose hue its app id picks.

## Where the dock's apps come from

The dock pins the *app entries* it finds when a desktop starts: files named
`share/slopos/apps/<id>.app`, each a few `key=value` lines:

```
id=org.slopos.editor
name=Editor
exec=/usr/local/bin/editor
icon=org.slopos.editor.png
```

`icon` names a PNG in the same share's `slopos/icons/`, and `boot=medium`
marks an app shown only when the computer started from the installer. Entries
are read from each extension's share, then `/usr/share` (the base),
`/usr/local/share` and `~/.local/share`; a later entry with the same id
replaces an earlier one, and the dock sorts them by name. An app's window is
matched to its entry by app id. [Installed software](/docs/architecture/software)
explains the places, and [Extensions](/docs/architecture/extensions) what an
extension's entries may do.

## The apps that ship

Some apps are part of the base, the read-only system every boot runs; the
rest are installed at `/usr/local`, outside the base, so they can be updated
on their own ([Installed software](/docs/architecture/software) explains
why).

| App | Program | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Terminal | `terminal` | base | A shell on a *pseudo-terminal* (a pair of endpoints that look like a serial line to the shell), emulating an xterm closely enough for full-screen programs and mouse reporting. Resizing re-wraps long lines in the scrollback; Ctrl+Shift+PgUp and PgDn scroll it. |
| System Monitor | `sysmon` | base | A *Performance* tab with a graph of the last minute for the processor, memory, each disk, each network adapter and the graphics adapter, and a *Processes* tab listing every process with its threads folded in, which can end one (through privd when it belongs to someone else). `sysmon --page cpu`, `memory`, `disk`, `network`, `gpu` or `processes` opens on a page. |
| Install SlopOS | `installer-ui` | base | The installer's window, shown only when the computer started from the installer ([Installing SlopOS](/docs/self-hosting/installing)). |
| Editor | `editor` | `/usr/local` | A text editor with tabs, a file tree, syntax highlighting, find and replace and a command palette. It saves through a synced copy renamed over the file, so a failed save never leaves half a file, and asks before replacing a file that changed since it was opened. Its manual is `share/slopos/doc/editor.md`. |
| Files | `file_manager` | `/usr/local` | A file manager with places in a sidebar, a toolbar and a table of the folder. |
| Image Viewer | `image_viewer` | `/usr/local` | Shows a PNG fitted to the window, zoomed with the wheel or `+` and `-`, `f` to fit again and `0` for actual size. |
| Widget Gallery | `widget_gallery` | `/usr/local` | Every appkit component in each of its states. It has no dock entry; start it from a terminal. |

## Looking at it

`just ui-shots` boots the live ISO under QEMU, plays scenes of keys, clicks
and commands typed at the serial console, and saves a PNG of each screen
under `builddir/ui-shots/`: each installer page, the consent dialog, the
system menu, the question *Power off…* asks, the clock's date, the lock and
login screens, the desktop with two windows and the pointer on the dock, each
app and each page of the gallery. Naming scenes takes only those (`just
ui-shots apps`; the scenes are `installer`, `chrome`, `desktop`, `apps` and
`gallery`), and `--no-install` stops the installer at its review page.
Nothing compares the shots with anything: they are for people to look at
before and after a change. The audit and the tests are the gate.

## For contributors

- **Name roles, never values.** An app names a `Tone`, a `Surface`, a
  `TextRole`, a `space` step or an `inset`, and appkit itself constructs no
  colour (the checks behind a picture's transparent pixels aside). A new role
  goes in `theme-core`, with a contrast test for every surface it may sit on.
- **A new component owns its behaviour.** Its states, focus ring, overflow and
  hit target are the component's job, and it joins the gallery
  (`appkit::catalogue`), so `appkit_test` audits it at three widths.
- **Words are data where they can be.** A surface whose words are data holds
  them to `theme_core::words` in a host test, and keeps trade words (device
  names, paths, sizes in bytes) in its details.
- **A new icon is a glyph of the shipped font.** Add an `Icon` variant naming
  Lucide's codepoint; the font test fails on one the font lacks. An app icon
  is a new `Art` in `tools/icons`, rendered with `just icons`.

## Further reading

- [Understanding SC 1.4.3 Contrast (Minimum)](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html)
  and [SC 1.4.11 Non-text Contrast](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-contrast.html).
  The two thresholds `theme-core`'s tests encode, explained with examples.
- [libadwaita's CSS variables](https://gnome.pages.gitlab.gnome.org/libadwaita/doc/main/css-variables.html).
  How GNOME's toolkit names its colours by surface and by meaning, the model
  for the theme's roles.
- [GNOME HIG: App Icons](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/app-icons.html)
  and [Apple HIG: App icons](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/app-icons).
  The icon rules `tools/icons` follows.
- [GNOME HIG: Writing style](https://developer.gnome.org/hig/guidelines/writing-style.html).
  Short, plain interface text and when a label takes an ellipsis.
- [*The Elm Architecture*](https://guide.elm-lang.org/architecture/). The
  model, view and update split appkit's `App` uses.
- [Design Tokens Format Module](https://www.designtokens.org/tr/2025.10/format/).
  The cross-tool vocabulary for named design values like `theme-core`'s.
- [Lucide's licence](https://lucide.dev/license). The icon font's terms, ISC
  with MIT for the icons derived from Feather.
